# Supply Chain Risk Management Notes

## Managing Vendor Relationships
- Ensure vendor security policies are at least as effective as your own
- Vendor management lifecycle
    1. Vendor selection 
        - Formal RFP
        - Informal process
        - Include security requirements
        - Include assessment of provider's risk management program
    2. Onboarding
        - Verify contract details
        - Arrange secure data transfer
        - Establish incident procedures
    3. Monitoring
        - Conduct site visits
        - Review independent audits
        - Handle security incidents
    4. Offboarding
        - Destroy confidential info
        - Unwind business relationship
        - Restart lifecycle
- ISO 27036-4

## Vendor Agreements
- Service level requirements (SLRs): Specific requirements that a customer has for any aspect of a vendor's service performance
- Document SLR in SLA
- Memorandum of Understanding (MoU)
- Memorandum of Agreement (MoA)
- Business Partners Agreement (BPA)
- Master Service Agreement (MSA)
- Work Order (WO)/Statement of Work (SoW)
- Include security requirements in SLRs, SLAs, and other agreements

## Cloud Audits
- System and Organization (SOC) reports offer an alterantive approach to evaluating providers.
    - SOC 1: Provides assurance required for customer financial audits
    - SOC 2: Provides detailed assurance of CIA triad controls (typically private)
    - SOC 3: Provides high-level, public reporting of CIA triad controls 
    - Type 1 Report: Describes the controls that the service provider has in place and an opinion on the suitability of those controls
    - Type 2 Report: Includes the same information as a Type 1 report along with the results of control testing by the auditor
    - SOC audits apply under SSAE 18 in the U.S. or ISAE 3402 internationally
